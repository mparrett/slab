# 38 — C-Beam 80: the Yamaha CS-80

`cs80` (C-Beam 80) is the Yamaha CS-80 of 1977, built from its circuit. The
CS-80 is the instrument of the Blade Runner soundtrack, Chariots of Fire and
a lot of other records from that era. It is eight notes of two complete
voices each, with polyphonic aftertouch. **Status:** built 2026-10-15 on
branch `worktree-cs80`.

Code:
- `kernels/06-voices/cs80.fy`: the voice.
- `machines/cs80/`: the panel and presets.
- `tools/cs80/factory_presets.py`: the presets, generated from Yamaha's
  preset charts.

## Sources

A research pass (2026-10-15) read Yamaha's own documents where they exist:

- the **Owner's Manual**: specifications on p.49, the preset charts on
  pp.28–29, and how each section behaves;
- the **CS-80 Adjustment Procedure** (36 pp., with Service News E-46):
  factory calibration voltages and times;
- the **CS-60 service manual's IC page**: Yamaha's data sheet for the
  IG00153, IG00158, IG00156 and IG00152 chips, which the CS-80 shares;
- the **CS-80 overall circuit diagram**, all 12 sheets.

Third-party sources were the Sound On Sound review of Deckard's Dream (written
with a CS-80 beside it), the Deckard's Dream, Arturia CS-80V and Cox manuals,
and Eddy Bergman's and Lanterman's IG00156 notes.

No paper models the IG00156, and Haible's SPICE analysis is offline. The
tables below mark each number:
- **[D]** comes from a Yamaha document;
- **[S]** was read off the schematic;
- **[3]** comes from a third party;
- **[G]** is our guess.

## What the circuit says

| part | behavior | |
|---|---|---|
| voices | 8 notes, each driving two M boards (Channel I, Channel II): 16 VCOs, 32 filter sections. MIX crossfades the channels; ring mod, chorus/tremolo and the outputs come after the mix | [D] |
| VCO, IG00153 | saw core, 3.5 Vpp. **Linear in Hz**: KV 0.25 V = 130.8 Hz, f ≈ 523.2 Hz × V. The key DAC makes the exponential voltages (lowest C 125 mV, ×1.0592 per key from a 4.000 V reference) | [D] |
| feet | 16', 8', 5⅓', 4', 2⅔', 2', switched currents into the FT pin. The filters get KV directly, so they follow the key and ignore the feet | [D][S] |
| waves, IG00158 | saw, pulse = saw > threshold (PW 50–90 %), sine from a shaped triangle. The sine **bypasses both filters** into the VCA | [D] |
| PWM | one sine LFO per channel, **shared by all eight voices**; 6.7 Hz at mid travel, 200 Hz at fast | [D][S] |
| noise | one zener source per channel, **shared by all voices**, with "significant low-frequency content" | [S][3] |
| filters, IG00156 | per voice an HPF then an LPF, each a 2-pole state-variable OTA filter (1.5 nF integrators). Q 0.5 at VQ 10 V, Q 5 at 0 V. **Does not self-oscillate**; at full resonance a pulse rings almost sinusoidally. 1 kHz at KV 0.25 V, Vf 5 V | [D] |
| VCF EG, IG00152 | bipolar: **IL** takes the output 0 → −5 V, **AL** 0 → +5 V. The note starts both cutoffs IL below the sliders, sweeps to AL above in A, decays to 0 (the sliders, the fixed sustain) in D, and on release falls back to IL in R. Both filters move together | [D] |
| EG times | exponential in control voltage, one octave of time per volt: A 1 ms–1 s, D and R 10 ms–10 s | [D] |
| VCA EG | ADSR plus LEVEL. It rests at −200 mV, under the VCA's zero: a dead zone that swallows small amounts | [D] |
| initial touch | a **decaying pulse** from the key's two contacts, not a static velocity. INITIAL BRILLIANCE/LEVEL act only while it lasts. TOUCH RESPONSE PITCH inverts it onto the VCOs: a hard note starts flat | [D] |
| after touch | **per note**. AFTER BRILLIANCE/LEVEL; TOUCH RESPONSE VCO and VCF set how much of the global sub oscillator each pressed note gets; SPEED raises the sub oscillator's rate. 6.4 V at full on the brilliance and level levers | [D] |
| sub oscillator | one for the instrument: sine, saw down, saw up, square, noise (no S&H), 0.7–60 Hz, to VCO, VCF and VCA | [D] |
| ring modulator | μA796 on the mixed output, a sine carrier 6.7 Hz (mid) to 200 Hz. An AD envelope raises the carrier frequency by DEPTH; it retriggers only after every key was up | [D][S] |
| chorus/tremolo | MN3001 dual BBD at 80–143 kHz clock (≈ 2–3 ms) mixed with the dry signal, plus AM VCAs on the stereo outputs. Chorus ≈ 0.5–5 Hz, tremolo ≈ 5–20 Hz; the level drops a little when on | [D][S] |
| keyboard control | brilliance and level, low and high levers, bipolar, rising smoothly toward each end; −2.76 V at the bottom key at full | [D] |
| portamento | the key code steps one semitone at a time at 0.17 ms–0.16 s per semitone, then an integrator smooths it: **constant rate**, time proportional to the interval. Glissando skips the integrator | [D] |
| drift | IG00153s were binned by drift over 25 → 50 °C: 0–45 cents at C1, 0–30 cents at C6; only matched ranks went into one instrument. The bass drifts more in cents, an offset in the linear core | [D] |
| CV bleed | the output VCA and the chorus VCAs have zeroing trims "for minimum deflection": an aged unit thumps | [D] |

## The voice

```
key ─ glide (semitone clock) ─┬─ × PITCH × feet × spread × drift × vibrato × IPB ─► IG00153 saw core (2×)
                              │     saw · pulse [PWM LFO, shared] · noise [shared] ─► coupling cap
                              │       ─► HPF (OTA SVF) ─► LPF (OTA SVF) ─► gentle pole
                              │       + sine (shaped triangle, past the filters)
                              │       ─► IG00151 VCA (dead zone, soft top)
                              └─ KV ─► both cutoffs, with the VCF EG, brilliance, keyboard control,
                                       touch and the sub oscillator, in volts
Channel I × MIX + Channel II × MIX ─► hb9 ─► ring mod ─► CV bleed ─► coupling ─► chorus/tremolo ─► L R
```

- **Two channels per voice.** Each Slab voice is one CS-80 note: two
  `CbChan` boards with their own envelopes, filters and spreads. `cb-chan`
  runs one channel for a sample; `cb-sub` runs its 2× substep.
- **The IG00156** (`cb-svf`) is a zero-delay SVF whose two integrators each
  pass their input through an OTA's tanh. A loud signal runs the
  integrators out of current, so the Q falls and the filter thickens
  instead of screaming. It can't self-oscillate: Q tops out at 5, as on the
  chip. The damping grows toward the top of the range (Q is frequency
  dependent, per Bergman), and a pole at 5× the LPF's cutoff gives the
  "gentle single-pole lowpass" he measured.
- **Cutoffs are volts.** fc = 1 kHz × (key / 130.8 Hz) × 2^((V − 5) × 0.85).
  V is the slider plus everything that moves both filters together. The
  0.85 octave per volt is our guess [G], calibrated so that slider travel
  spans the audio range at C2 and the manual's presets land where their
  names say.
- **The VCF EG** (in `cb-chan`) keeps volts, not a 0..1 level, so IL → AL →
  0 → IL is literal. The attack aims 20 % past AL like an RC's knee. A
  voice that has died away starts again at IL.
- **Global sources share one clock.** The CS-80 has one sub oscillator,
  one PWM LFO per channel, one noise per channel and one ring carrier.
  Slab's voices can't see each other, so the host now passes its sample
  clock to the kernels (`ctx.clock`, docs/04): `cb-block-prepare` carries
  each shared phase across the samples since its last call. Each voice
  counts its own samples from the clock at its note-on, so all eight
  compute the same phase and the same noise sample (`clock-hash`, a Weyl step
  through two quadratic folds, flat to 0.1 dB).
- **Per-note state the instrument shares** lives in params, written by the
  note hooks:
  - `gates` (which voices are held) gives the ring modulator its "retrigger
    once every key is up" rule;
  - `prs` (each voice's pressure) gives AFTER SPEED its hardest-pressed key;
  - `last-oct`, `grp-clock` and `grp-from` let every note of a chord glide
    from the same previous note;
  - `gen` (the note group) lets SUSTAIN II end the previous chord's
    die-away.
- **Chorus per voice.** A BBD on the mix is linear in its input, so it is
  run per voice on a host `buffer` with the shared LFO: two taps in
  antiphase, 12 kHz reconstruction poles, and AM in antiphase. The cost is
  that buffers turn off host unison for this machine; the CS-80 already
  doubles every note.

## Guesses and limits

- **[G] Values we chose:**
  - cutoff 0.85 octave per volt;
  - the initial touch pulse falls in about 0.25 s;
  - after touch lags the pad by about 20 ms;
  - full-scale depths for sub VCO (±1 octave), sub VCF (±3 V) and sub VCA
    (100 %);
  - TOUCH RESPONSE VCO (±1 semitone), VCF (±2 V) and PITCH (half the
    frequency);
  - the ring envelope reaches up 4 octaves;
  - chorus depths;
  - the BBD's 12 kHz filters.
- **Pressure** rests at 0.5 in Slab's note expression (docs/22). After
  touch is the part above that, `(p − 0.5) × 2`, which also stands in for
  the pad's threshold.
- **Not modelled:**
  - the ribbon: use note-expression pitch, which bends one note rather than
    all of them;
  - the EXP/WAH pedal;
  - the sub oscillator's EXT input;
  - BBD hiss and clock residue;
  - the memories;
  - the original's refusal to steal a ninth voice: Slab steals the oldest.
- The sub oscillator's noise is raw white noise, as on the original. To
  vibrato that is breath rather than wobble.

## Panel and presets

The VOICE page holds Channel I over Channel II in the CS-80's slider order:
- VCO: FEET, PWM SPEED, PWM, PW, SQR, SAW, NOISE;
- VCF: HPF, RES, LPF, RES, IL, AL, A, D, R;
- VCA: VCF, SINE, A, D, S, R, LEVEL;
- TOUCH: four knobs, so a row fits the default window.

The CONTROL page holds the levers under the keyboard lid. Sliders read 0–10
like the original's; times are seconds.

| preset | what it is |
|---|---|
| brass, brass-3, strings, flute, e-piano, bass, harpsichord | Yamaha's factory pairings, from the Owner's Manual charts read to half a division |
| rain-brass | two detuned BRASS 1s, pressure brings brilliance, level and vibrato: the Blade Runner end-titles method. Put a long hall after it |
| tears-pad | STRING 2 over a slow PWM layer with chorus |
| ring-bells | the manual's bells: sines and saw, the ring envelope sweeping its carrier |
| solo-lead | portamento at the constant rate, pressure opens the filter and adds vibrato |

No authenticated panel settings exist for the Blade Runner sounds; rain-brass
is reconstructed from Yamaha's brass method.

## Cost

ReleaseFast bench, 2026-10-15: an 8-note chord takes 781 ns per sample
(3.75 % of a core), about 98 ns per sounding voice slot with NEON voice
pairs. That is about Profit-5's cost with twice its oscillators and filters.
The kernel stays at 16 `ifte` bodies (sub-oscillator sine, noise on, ring
on, chorus on). The per-channel wave switches multiply instead of branching,
so the versioned bodies stay under fy's cap of 32.
